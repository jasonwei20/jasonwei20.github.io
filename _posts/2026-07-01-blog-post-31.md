# Work

Recently I started thinking about pretraining data as a lever for improving models. The biggest things that I learned to think about while doing this work is:
What is being exercised by the data? Would doing well at that task improve performance at other tasks?
What is the “information importance” of that data? For instance, a paper describing the learnings from a series of raw experiments may be more important than the experiments itself, because even though the model is technically learning facts about the world from those experiments, it may not be the important facts about the world to learn. 
How can we use AI to accelerate scientific innovation? What parts of innovation are actually bottlenecked by intelligence, and what parts are actually bottlenecked by knowledge, data, or real-life experiments?

Half dome. Half dome was the most challenging hike I have done in my life. The hike started butt early in the morning at 5:45am and lasted until almost 8pm in the evening, totalling 44 thousand steps. I learned that I could push my body pretty far with the right nutrition and preparation, and that knee issues that I suffered from before were largely gone. The hike up was fun and rewarding. Many people find the cables to be the most challenging part, however I felt that they were relatively safe given that you could use your hands to prevent falls. I actually felt that the part going up to the dome that was not as steep but did not have cables was far more dangerous. The hike down was far worse than the hike up as the sun came out and every step was harsh on the knees. Overall I probably would not do it again and I don’t think it was worth the high risk of injury, but I am glad to have had the experience.
